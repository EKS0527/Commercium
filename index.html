Resources: Glossary

Included are definitions, formulas and other pieces of information to improve understanding:

Options

Options: A contract that gives the buyer the right, however not the obligation, to buy/sell an underlying asset at a set price prior to a predetermined date.

  • Call Options: The financial contract that grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy a specific amount of an underlying asset at a determined price before a specific date.

  • Put Options: The financial contract that grants the buyer the right, but not the obligation, to sell a specific amount of an underlying asset at a determined price before a specific date.
Key Options & Market Terms
  • Implied Volatility: The standard deviation of stock returns that is consistent with an option's market value.

  • Moneyness: The relationship between the price of the underlying asset and an option's exercise price.

  • Securities: Tradeable financial assets that corporations and governments use to raise capital from investors.

  • Strike Price: Price at which stock or commodity underlying a call option or a put option can be purchased or sold during a specified period.

  • Time to Expiration: Duration until the option contract expires.

  • Underlying Stock Price: The current live market price of the specific stock or equity upon which a derivative contract—such as an option, warrant, or convertible security—is based.

  • Volatility: Indicates how much and how quickly the value of an investment, market, or market sector changes.

  • Volatility Smile: The variation of implied volatility with strike price.
The Greeks
  • Delta: Measures how much an option's price is expected to change per a $1.00 change in the price of an underlying stock. Call options range between 0 and +1.00, while put options range from 0 to -1.00. It also roughly estimates the probability of expiring in the money.

  • Gamma: Rate of change in Delta when underlying stock moves by $1.00 (second derivative of option price). High gamma means Delta can shift very quickly, which is common for at-the-money options near expiration.

  • Rho: Measures an option's sensitivity to changes in interest rates. It estimates how much an option's price will change for every 1% change in the risk-free interest rate.

  • Theta: Measures the rate of time decay in an option's value as it gets closer to its expiration date, assuming all other factors remain constant. Theta is typically negative for option buyers because the option loses value each day.

  • Vega: Measures an option's sensitivity to changes in the implied volatility of the underlying asset. It represents how much an option's price is expected to change for every 1% change in implied volatility.
